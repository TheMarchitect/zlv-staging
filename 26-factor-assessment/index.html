The Problem

"Improve your financials. Reduce owner dependency." You've heard this before.

You've probably heard this advice before. It's not wrong. It's just incomplete — and incomplete advice on a decision this size is expensive. Every business has a different value profile. A trucking company and a software firm have completely different leverage points. The factors that add three multiples to one business might barely move the needle on another. Generic advice applied to a specific business produces generic results. Here's what standard valuations miss: they apply generic multiples to generic metrics — revenue, EBITDA, industry comps. What they don't examine is the specific combination of operational strength, market position, and strategic factors that make your business worth more or less than the comparable. That gap — between what a generic valuation captures and what's actually there — is where most founders leave their money.

The Three Tiers

Operational. Market. Value Accelerators. Three lenses. One complete picture.

The 26 factors are organized into three tiers — each examining a different dimension of value that sophisticated buyers evaluate before making an offer.

TIER 01
Operational Drivers
The internal fundamentals. Does this business actually work — and will it keep working after the founder leaves? Factors scored: Financials, Growth Strategy, Technology, Operations, Customer Satisfaction, Senior Management, Human Resources, Legal, Innovation. Most founders score lower here than they expect. Not because the business doesn't run well — but because "runs well" and "documented well enough to survive acquisition due diligence" are different things.
TIER 02
Market Drivers
The external signals. Is this business positioned to grow — and will that growth hold after the acquisition? Factors scored: Growth Trajectory, Market Opportunity, Dominant Market Share, Business Model, Recurring Revenue, Barriers to Entry, Product Differentiation, Brand, Margin Advantage, Customer Diversification. This is where most of the hidden value lives. Market repositioning alone has taken businesses from sub-1x multiples to 7x — not because the business changed, but because the right buyer saw the right picture.
TIER 03
Value Accelerators
The multipliers. In today's acquisition market, these increasingly separate a competitive offer from an extraordinary one. Factors scored: Impact Mission, Social Intelligence, Data Strategies, Diversity Policies, Activated Culture. Most advisors never examine these factors. Which is exactly why they leave them — and the multiples attached to them — on the table.
The Four Principles

The 26 factors are built on four ideas that most advisors never tell you.

01
Lower Risk Equals Higher Value
Every factor we optimize reduces the buyer's perceived risk. Risk and value are inverse — reduce one and the other goes up automatically. This is why operational improvements and market positioning aren't just "nice to have." They are the mechanism of value creation.
02
Valuation and Transaction Value Are Not the Same
Your business may be worth $50 million by every reasonable measure. But what a buyer actually pays depends on how the deal is structured, how the negotiation goes, and how well-prepared you are to defend your number.
03
Companies Are Sold, Not Bought
Buyers don't show up and rescue founders who are ready to exit. The exits that produce real wealth are engineered — positioned, timed, and executed deliberately. Waiting for the right buyer to find you is a strategy for leaving money on the table.
04
Exit Strategy Is Growth Strategy
Every improvement you make in preparation for a sale makes your business better regardless of whether the sale happens. Better operations. Stronger management. Cleaner finances. The process pays for itself.

What the Score Shows You

Most businesses score lower than their owners expect. That's actually good news.

In our experience, the first 26-factor assessment almost always reveals gaps the founder hadn't seen — or had seen but didn't know how to quantify. A low score on customer diversification might mean one contract is quietly suppressing your multiple by two full turns. A low score on recurring revenue might mean a simple restructuring of your billing model could add millions to your exit price. A low score on management depth might mean the most valuable thing you can do in the next 12 months isn't a marketing initiative — it's hiring a COO. The score doesn't judge your business. It shows you where the levers are. Knowing the gaps is valuable. But knowing which gaps matter most, in what sequence to address them, and how to close them while still running a business — that's where most founders get stuck. That's the work we do together.
